## Breakthrough
"No gatekeeping, no approval process. Push directly to main. If something breaks, we fix it together."

This isn't just a repo policy — it's a design principle for how engineering teams should share knowledge and tooling. Echoing the Internet's founding spirit: open, share, and contribute together.

## Why This Matters
Most internal tooling dies because of friction: approval processes, code review gates, "talk to the owner first." The tools that actually get adopted are the ones where anyone can improve them without asking permission. The bug bash repo is an experiment in this — structured enough to be useful, open enough to evolve.

## When to Apply
When building any shared team resource (repos, runbooks, skills, test infrastructure):
- Default to open, not locked down
- Make contributing as easy as consuming
- Trust the team — if something breaks, fix it together
- Frame it as "let's make it useful together" not "here's what I built, use it"

## Connection to Engineering Principles
- **Stewardship**: Leave systems better than you found them
- **Humility**: You don't have all the answers — the team does, collectively
- **Gratitude**: Respect what came before. Build on it, don't replace it
